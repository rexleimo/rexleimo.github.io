Anthropic May Land at 'Double B': The AI Books Get Their First Counterfeit Check

In its $63B chip financing, Blackstone told investors Anthropic will probably come out of the IPO in the 'double B' range (top of junk). Its own last forecast was investment-grade by 2027. I read the FT reporting via TechCrunch, Stoller's S-1 teardown, and the 322-point HN thread. Three things stuck: ratings are just a counterfeit check on the AI books; the genuinely odd figure in the S-1 is how often third-party companies are mentioned; and the Amazon analogy is the most level-headed footnote in the story.

October 31, 2026 · 7 min · 1373 words · DreamBeast Dev -  |  中文

I spent an afternoon working through the FT rating story (as relayed by TechCrunch), the long S-1 teardown from Stoller’s shop, and the 322-point Hacker News thread. What stings is not the junk rating itself — it is the Amazon analogy somebody pressed into it: Amazon carried a speculative-grade rating for well over a decade (HN reader tptacek, as quoted in the thread). I follow the order I actually read things, tag every number to a source, and say plainly where I did not get to a primary document.

The punchline first#

Blackstone told investors inside Anthropic’s $63B chip financing that post-IPO, Anthropic will most likely land in the “double B” range — top of junk (FT report, relayed in full by TechCrunch). Flip that against Blackstone’s own previous forecast: investment grade by 2027 (same source chain). So the news is not “AI companies are getting downgraded.” It is “the anchor investor walked back its own stated expectation.” After working through the reporting, I kept three things: the rating is just one counterfeit check on the AI books; the genuinely anomalous number in the S-1 is how often third-party companies are mentioned, not the loss itself; and the calmest line in the thread is the one comparing Anthropic to Amazon.

Why “double B” is the notch that matters#

Pin the vocabulary first. The investment-grade line is BBB- and up in S&P/Fitch, Baa3 and up at Moody’s; below that is speculative grade — junk, in street slang — with BB+/Ba1 as its top rung (Wikipedia “Bond credit rating” scale; S&P’s own glossary defines BB as speculative). TechCrunch’s relay of FT puts Anthropic in the “double B range, top of speculative” (TechCrunch, Oct 30, 2:52 PM PDT).

Investment grade and junk grade are not adjectives; they are two different funding channels. Junk grade means bond yields jump a step and it becomes “harder and more expensive to keep feeding the heavy spend that the business runs on” (TechCrunch, paraphrasing the report). Anthropic has spent the last twelve months on compute procurement (TechCrunch), and somebody keeps paying that bill.

The odd figure in the S-1 is how often others get named#

The Stoller-side piece (BIG by Matt Stoller, written by Lee Hepner, around Oct 31; the task’s slug 404’d — the real one has an extra “i,” retitled “Anthropic Isn’t Just a Company, It’s a Payoff”) is not about ratings. It is about taking the S-1 apart. Its count of named third-party companies: Amazon 153, Google 109, Spark Capital 39, Broadcom 36 (fig 2, sourced to Anthropic Form S-1, 2026-10-26). His line: “I’ve read a lot of S-1s and I’ve never seen one where the IPO company talks about how much it depends on other big companies” (quoting Stoller’s piece).

His own control group, Google’s 2004 S-1: top entry Silicon Graphics at 16 mentions, Yahoo 14, Microsoft 13 (fig 3, sourced to Google Form S-1, 2004-04-29). And Amazon’s 1997 filing: top entry Coast Wide Supply, 28. Same method, one order of magnitude apart.

Stoller’s read: Amazon/Google/Microsoft are simultaneously Anthropic’s distributors (roughly 50% of product sold through them), shareholders (Google ~15%, Amazon 10–12%), and lenders (Amazon’s multibillion convertible, Broadcom’s $42B-guaranteed structure) — “four monopolies in one trench coat” (Stoller, quoting the S-1’s risk section). The “four monopolies” framing is his argument, not the S-1’s language; I write it as his frame, not as fact.

Laying the books out: the loss is the known quantity, the rating the new one#

The numbers I could pin down, all source-tagged:

The easy place to get pulled around: the S-1 itself discloses a 2025 net loss of $41.97B (EDGAR), but Stoller writes “Anthropic lost $8B last year” (BIG paraphrase). That gap is nearly a factor of two, and I could not trace the $8B to a source — honest-boundaries treatment: I use only the S-1 figure in the body and flag the $8B in the boundaries note.

The Amazon analogy is the calmest footnote in the story#

In the HN thread (322 points; the task snapshot said 287 comments; my full Algolia tree pull shows 263 visible, the difference treated as deleted/private), tptacek’s “Amazon carried junk for over a decade” got pushed up. I stopped at that: at its listing Amazon was also loss-making, also cash-burning on heavy assets, also ugly on paper — and it sat in speculative grade for well over a decade before climbing to investment grade. cmiles8 said it blunter: “By any normal business logic, all these AI labs look awful on paper; any loan should be speculative” (HN paraphrase). dofm caught the wording: “the real bomb is the ‘may not’” (HN paraphrase) — Blackstone says it “may not” get the grade, not that it won’t; the FT piece is a forecast, not an agency action.

deepwoods added the maturity mismatch: first models in late 2022, Claude in March 2023 — “the industry is in year 4, and bond people think in 20-year terms” (HN paraphrase). That mismatch is the ground floor of a junk rating, not “AI is a scam.”

Honest boundaries: what I did not verify, said plainly#

The FT original (59383c7f) sits behind a paywall; the 270KB page I pulled got blocked and I never read it in full — every FT number in this piece runs through TechCrunch’s relay or HN readers quoting the FT lede. Stoller’s “$8B loss” versus the $41.97B I read off the S-1 on EDGAR: I could not source the $8B, so the body carries only the S-1 number. The $1.5–2T IPO valuation is secondhand (WSJ/Fortune); I did not read their originals, so I cite the source and do not hard-code the number. OpenAI’s parallel funding round (WSJ) came as a background contrast to the task; I did not pull that piece and put no specific number in the body. Amazon’s 1997 S-1 “Coast Wide Supply, 28 mentions” comes from Stoller’s chart; I did not check the 1997 original. And whether the rating actually lands is, for now, Blackstone’s forecast — S&P, Moody’s, and Fitch announcements govern.

One-line close#

The headline says “Anthropic may get a junk rating.” What I kept is not the “may.” It is that the anchor investor flipped its own stated expectation: Blackstone last round said investment-grade by 2027, this round says double B post-IPO (FT via TechCrunch). In a market with a $63B chip financing, a $1.5–2T valuation, and a $965B private mark sitting on the same table, what a rating agency hands you is not a judgment. It is a counterfeit check. The numbers it counts are more honest than the stock price.

Are you watching AI-company debt?#

If you follow AI-company credit or are reading an S-1 like Anthropic’s, drop a note: which name you are watching, and why you think it does not deserve investment grade. What I filed from this “double B reversal” is not the rating itself but the act of Blackstone flipping its own expectation — who is the next one to flip? Talk in the comments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 'double B range' mean?

Double B is S&P's BB (Moody's Ba1), the top rung of speculative-grade (junk) ratings. One notch up, BBB-, is the bottom of investment grade. TechCrunch's relay of the FT report puts Anthropic's likely post-IPO rating in that band.

Have the agencies actually downgraded Anthropic?

No. This is Blackstone's forecast to investors inside the chip financing, reported by FT and relayed by TechCrunch. S&P, Moody's, and Fitch announcements are the final word.

How big is Anthropic's loss?

The S-1 (pulled from EDGAR) shows a 2025 net loss of $41.97B against $4.585B of revenue. Stoller's article says '$8B' for last year's loss; I could not trace that figure to its source, so I use only the S-1 number.

Why does it matter that Amazon is mentioned 153 times in the S-1?

It shows where the filing's risk narrative actually centers: Anthropic's dependence on the four cloud/chip majors as distributors, shareholders, and lenders. By the same counting method, Google's 2004 S-1 topped out at 16 mentions and Amazon's 1997 filing at 28.
