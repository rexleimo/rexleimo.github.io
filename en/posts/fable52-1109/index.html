Fable 5.2 formalizes Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours: 20% price cut, Pro usage up to 1.5x

Anthropic shipped Fable 5.2 on Nov 9: 20% API price cut, 65 on the Artificial Analysis intelligence index, RLI more than doubled, and 1,000 agents formalizing Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours. The cost ledger and the Pro freebie, itemized.

November 9, 2026 · 10 min · 2042 words · 梦兽 -  |  中文

November 9, Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.2. The hardest number first: a thousand Fable agents formalized the proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem in thirty hours, machine-checkable, step by step. The same thousand-agent setup on Opus 5.5 got 26% done in eighteen hours, then mostly stopped. The previous formalization, done by an unreleased research build, took eleven days. Thirty hours is not a marketing claim; it is a completed event.

The release bundles three things: a price cut, a new effort tier, and free usage for subscribers. Read the three together, not separately: the price cut hits the API side, the freebie hits the subscription side, the new tier hits the cost-performance side. This post does the arithmetic.

The price cut is a real cut#

The API went from $10 per million input tokens and $50 output on 5.1 to $8 and $40: 20% off on both sides. A new fast mode: $16/$80, up to 2.5x generation speed. Normal generation is up to 30% faster than 5.1.

Cache pricing moved too: cache reads from 25 cents to 20 cents, cache writes for the 5-minute and 1-hour tiers to $10 and $16 (the price table VentureBeat pulled from the System Card). Heavy cache users see the real cost drop more than 20%.

A price cut on the most expensive model is a signal: Fable is not a limited edition, it is meant to be used. Note that Opus 5.5 lists even cheaper, $4/$20. The lineup now has three clear lanes: cheap for daily work, expensive for hard problems, and the tiered-effort play in between.

Where the scores went#

The Remote Labor Index (RLI), which grades the model on real remote freelance work, jumped the most: 17.9% on 5.1 to 41.3% at High effort on 5.2, more than double, well clear of Opus 5.5’s 21.3%. On FrontierCode 2.0 for agentic coding, 49.1% at Xhigh effort (5.1: 32.4%, Opus: 45.5%). Robotics simulation RoboDojo went from 27.6% on 5.1 to 64.9%.

On the independent side, Artificial Analysis’s intelligence index puts Fable 5.2 at full effort at 65, the top of the board, five points ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon and six ahead of Opus 5.5; the previous generation’s 5.1 sat at 49.

Every benchmark figure comes from Anthropic’s announcement; the ranking figures come from Artificial Analysis’s public board. What is transcribed from charts is labeled in the honest boundaries.

But Fable does not sweep every benchmark: on the business-workflows AutomationBench, Gemini 4 Carbon’s 53.9 sits above Fable’s 51.4, and GPT-6’s Astra posts 42.8 on FrontierCode. The board structure is first on overall intelligence with individual losses elsewhere; when you quote the numbers, look at the columns, not just your favorite row.

A few more pinned: OSWorld computer use at 89.3% (5.1: 83.5%); agentic search WANDR at 82.1% against Opus’s 72.3%; chart recognition Chartography leaping from 44.8% on 5.1 to 73.4%, the biggest single jump in the table. An external user’s remark that 5.2 draws images in JavaScript that rival dedicated image models lands on exactly that chart score.

Cost performance: two official points, one external re-test#

Two points on Anthropic’s own charts matter most. One: Fable 5.2 at High effort matches Opus 5.5 at Max on coding scores for roughly half the cost. Two: at the lowest tier (Low) on Humanity’s Last Exam it ties Opus 5.5 at Max for less than a tenth of the cost.

Those two points are the core of the release: the expensive model is not meant to be run wide open; it is meant to be run a tier down.

External data fills the rest. On Artificial Analysis’s public board, Max effort costs about $26.50 per task, the rightmost point on the cost chart; High effort scores 62 at $9.08 per task, which is better and cheaper than Opus 5.5’s Max (59 at $10.04). One developer running their own agent pipeline re-tested on 30 routine coding tasks: Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5 both solved 26; 5.1 solved 25. For daily coding, the two are effectively indistinguishable.

The argument is in the tokens: external measurements put output tokens per task up from 118k on 5.1 to 274k on 5.2, with 192k of that in reasoning tokens. Scores go up, but tokens go up more, and the diminishing-returns case has receipts. Whether it pays depends on the job: open-ended hard work buys back the reasoning spend; routine coding does not. One more number from the System Card (via VentureBeat): $6.72 average cost per task on Cognition’s FrontierCode 2.0 at High effort.

What it could not do before, it can now#

The Fermat thirty hours is the headline. The details are rarer still: the agents got a single list of intermediate theorem statements to score progress on, were not allowed to consult mathematical references, and figured out their own coordination. They used a third of the action tokens of the first formalization and produced a proof seven times shorter. Same category: an engineer handed 5.2 an empty folder, and it coordinated over a thousand subagents for eight days straight to produce a bootable laptop OS prototype, with drivers, filesystem, windows, login screen, and installer.

The hardware line is the weirder one: three devices, a home voice-assistant hub, a USB speakerphone, a handheld virtual pet. The model wrote its own spec, picked the parts, routed the four-layer boards, modeled the enclosures, and programmed the firmware; each board was manufacturing-ready in under a day, and Diode built them back with zero electrical modifications.

Theoretical physics closed two open black-hole problems, and the results are out as preprints. Protein structure: 28 of 34 AAV capsids predicted accurately. Internally, a critical production service migrated from Python to Rust now starts 26 times faster.

One easily missed line: unnecessary blocks are down roughly 55%. The previous generation had a habit of intercepting ordinary requests as dangerous ones; the official claim is that the false-positive rate was cut by more than half.

The model also picks up two new roles: Advisor, where Haiku, Sonnet, or Opus go to it when stuck; and Orchestrator, where it delegates the smaller tasks to cheaper models. Anthropic’s placement is explicit: daily work on Opus 5.5, switch up to Fable for the hard problems, step down to Sonnet or Haiku for the easy ones.

Mythos: same model, different pants#

Fable and Mythos share one underlying model. The difference is the guardrails: Mythos has part of its cybersecurity and biology limits removed and is restricted to vetted institutions, through Anthropic’s Cyber and Life Sciences verification programs.

The cyber numbers: CyScenarioBench at 76.3% for Mythos 5.2, against 61.7% for 5.1; one security company found that one in six confirmed vulnerabilities Mythos found was missed by every other frontier model they tested. On the product side there is Claude Security: Enterprise customers can run the strongest model continuously over codebases, track vulnerability fixes, and wire it into their tooling via API and MCP. The cyber line now runs from the model all the way to the product.

Timing is another layer: per CNBC, this is the first frontier model Anthropic has shipped since Dario Amodei’s September call to slow the frontier pace, and the company’s possible IPO is about a week out. The model is the product; the product is going on stage.

The 20% API price cut is the other half of the same ledger: the flagship tier is being put into the market, and the whole price list is getting denser.

How to claim the Pro freebie#

The freebie is an official promotion, two plan classes, two rule sets, all in the official support doc.

Pro class: from November 9 through November 16 Pacific time (deadline 11-17 15:59 Beijing), up to 50% of your weekly usage limit can go to Fable models at no extra charge, plus one free reset card.

A reset card touches two limits. It restores the Fable portion of your weekly limit, leaving other models’ spend untouched, and it refills the entire five-hour window, including other models’ spend. Anthropic’s own example: 80% of the weekly limit used, 50% of it Fable and 30% other models, a reset brings you back to 30%.

The community version of the arithmetic is easier: weekly limit of 100, Fable capped at 50. Spend 50 on Fable, 50 on Opus, and the week is empty. Hit the reset and the Fable 50 comes off the counter; your usable total for the week becomes 150, 1.5x normal.

Two details: the official doc itself notes Fable burns limits faster than other models, so do not empty it in the first three days; and a reset is irreversible, canceling or downgrading voids it.

Max and Ultra are different: Fable was already in their 50% weekly allowance, so there is no one-week window, just free cards. Card count by subscription length: under six months gets one, six months to a year gets two, over a year gets three, valid until March 8, 2027. Eligibility is a paid bill in place before 10:00 am Pacific on November 9; subscription length counts from your first paid bill, and a gap in the middle does not reset it.

After the week ends, Fable goes back to usage-credit billing on Pro, the unused 50% does not carry over, and an unused card lapses. Before 11-17 the freebie is a real freebie; after that line, Fable bills by the token again.

Honest boundaries#

The two points “High matches Opus Max at half the cost” and “Low ties Opus Max on HLE at under a tenth of the cost” are transcriptions from the charts in the official announcement; this machine never ran the model. The Max tier’s $26.50 per task, High’s 62 at $9.08, and the 274k output tokens per task are all external public data; no original evals were reproduced. The 26/26/25 on thirty tasks is a self-reported community re-test, not verified line by line. Every number in the benchmark table is self-reported by the releasing party, and the announcement states plainly that no external organization reviewed them.

Frequently Asked Questions#

Is Fable 5.2 worth it for daily work? Anthropic’s own recommendation is Opus 5.5 for daily coding and knowledge work, Fable for the hard problems. External re-testing agrees: 30 routine coding tasks, 5.2 and Opus 5.5 both at 26. Fable’s value is open-ended hard work, not replacing the daily driver.

What’s the difference between Fable and Mythos? Same model, two safeguard tiers. Fable keeps all the safeguards and is available to everyone; Mythos goes only through institutional verification. If you do defensive security work: Fable’s guardrails will block most cyber tasks by design, not bug; that work needs the Mythos channel.

How do I maximize the Pro card? Fable up to the 50% cap, then Opus until the weekly limit is empty, reset, then Fable again. Three passes, 150% of the week. Deadline 11-17 15:59 Beijing time; the reset is irreversible, and canceling or downgrading voids it.

Is “30% faster” real? The official claim is up to 30% over 5.1, plus a 2.5x fast mode at double the price. External reports say 5.2 fixed 5.1’s slowness and overflow problems; nobody has re-measured the 30% itself.

What’s the IPO background? CNBC: first frontier model since Dario’s “pace” call, IPO possibly next week. Background, not a forecast.

Which Side Are You On#

The core of this release is an arithmetic question: the flagship tier costs twice as much, but running it a tier down saves money. One camp is the cost-performance camp: 90% of daily tasks on Opus 5.5, Fable only at Low/High effort on the 10% that is genuinely hard. The other camp is the frontier camp: Max wide open, $26.50 a task, consider it the price of admission.

Which tier are you running, and does the usage math work out for you? Say so in the post.

The arithmetic is worth forwarding to the colleague who has been burning Fable at full effort. What that person lacks is not a stronger model; it is the habit of running a tier down.
