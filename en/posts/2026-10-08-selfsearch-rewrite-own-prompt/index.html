SelfSearch: Letting Agents Rewrite Their Own Prompts — Matching Codex for $4.03

SelfSearch (arXiv 2609.37968): without any downstream reward, agents rewrite their own instructions/tools/procedures using records of prior self-modifications, and a $4.03 search cost yields a harness that solves 82.0% of Terminal-Bench 2.1 — matching Codex, the top of a public nine-harness comparison. The extreme version of prompt evolution.

October 8, 2026 · 4 min · 821 words · 梦兽编程 -  |  中文

Prompt evolution has been a craft: change the prompt on a hunch, run a batch, let gut decide the next version. SelfSearch (arXiv 2609.37968) turns it into a search problem: the agent doesn’t modify code, it modifies itself — rewriting its own instructions, tools, and execution procedures, grounded in “records of its earlier self-modification episodes,” with no task-level reward anywhere. Result: a $4.03 search cost produces a harness that solves 82.0% of Terminal-Bench 2.1, matching Codex, the top of a public nine-harness comparison. This post pulls every number out of the official abstract and says precisely where it diverges from “hand-tuning prompts.”

The verdict in three numbers#

  1. $4.03 → 82.0%: a reward-free search at $4.03 cost yields a harness that, under the settings of a public nine-harness comparison (DeepSeek V4 Flash), solves 82.0% of Terminal-Bench 2.1, matching Codex, the top scorer in that comparison.
  2. Single-agent gain up to +11.2pp: population-mean success rises in all six model-benchmark settings; on SWE-bench Multilingual, +5.0pp with execution cost -38.5% on tasks both versions solve — the evolved harness is not just stronger, it’s cheaper.
  3. The fuel is “experience,” not “reward”: no downstream task reward during search; the agent runs on records of its earlier self-modifications (reasoning + tool actions + outcomes). That’s exactly why it’s cheap.

The mechanism: an agent rewrites itself, records as experience#

The official abstract, term by term:

  • LLM agents can already inspect and modify their own instructions, tools, and execution procedures (the harness). Prior work searches for better agents by repeated downstream evaluation — “incurs substantial costs and ties the search to the evaluated tasks.”
  • SelfSearch’s change: reward-free — when the agent modifies itself, it reads the record of its earlier self-modification attempts. Each record holds three things: reasoning, tool actions, outcomes. The modified agent becomes the next round’s improver.
  • Effect: population-mean success up in all six settings; single agents +11.2pp on Terminal-Bench 2.1; SWE-bench Multilingual +5.0pp with -38.5% cost; $4.03 to a harness that matches Codex.

In plain terms: hand-tuning a prompt is “change one version, run a batch, read the score” — the score is the reward, expensive and task-bound. SelfSearch is “write down how the last few rounds modified themselves, why, and what came out; feed that into the next round” — no task score needed as guidance, which is why it’s cheap ($4.03-class) and task-agnostic.

Why “prompts” are the main character#

What the model actually reads to decide behavior in a harness is, at bottom, prompt + tool descriptions + procedure conventions. What SelfSearch modifies — “its own instructions, tools, and execution procedures” — is the prompt family, all of it. So the results land squarely on prompt engineering:

  • Prompts are an automatable, searchable engineering object, not just hand-written text;
  • The search’s fuel is records of self-modification (including failures), not downstream task reward;
  • A byproduct is efficiency: the evolved harness spends less money on tasks both versions solve (-38.5%) — better prompting isn’t only higher success, it’s lower inference cost.

Read with the companion Vercel piece: one subtraction, one evolution#

The first piece in this batch (Vercel’s 14 rules) is humans doing prompt subtraction: a 125-line prompt bloats to 1,000, then gets cut to 14 deterministic rules in code with the model left only for judgment. SelfSearch is agents evolving prompts: no human in the loop — the agent searches its own modifications using its own record. Together they cover the two directions of prompt engineering: cut what’s deterministic into rules; evolve what’s judgment with search. Both treat the prompt as a maintainable, measurable, automatable engineering object.

Honest note#

  • Not reproduced locally: no Terminal-Bench 2.1 environment and no DeepSeek V4 Flash access. The 82.0% / +11.2pp / $4.03 are self-reported numbers from the paper’s official abstract (arXiv 2609.37968v2, 2026-09-30); I checked each against the abstract but did not re-run anything.
  • “$4.03 matches Codex” is under the public nine-harness comparison’s settings — different benchmark or model tier, and the claim may not hold; Codex is the top scorer in that run, not the strongest harness in absolute terms.
  • Paper v1 (09-29) → v2 (09-30), one day apart; numbers above are the v2 abstract.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 'reward-free' mean?

No task-level reward signal during search. The agent's basis for self-modification is its 'record of earlier self-improvement episodes' — what was changed, the reasoning, the tool actions, the outcomes — fed into the next round of modification.

What exactly is the $4.03?

Total cost of the search phase (evolving the harness), not the cost of the 82.0% run itself. After a $4.03 search, the resulting harness solves 82.0% of Terminal-Bench 2.1 with DeepSeek V4 Flash under the settings of a public nine-harness comparison, matching the top-scoring Codex.

How big is the improvement?

Population-mean success rises in all six model-benchmark settings; single agents gain up to +11.2pp on Terminal-Bench 2.1. On SWE-bench Multilingual: +5.0pp, and -38.5% execution cost on tasks solved by both versions.

What's the value over me tuning prompts by hand?

Hand tuning is one hypothesis per round; SelfSearch is a search — it batches trials using records of prior self-modifications (including failures) as an experience bank, and the search cost ($4.03) sits well below evaluation-guided baselines. Prompts go from craft to engineering object.

Can I use this directly?

No Terminal-Bench setup and no DeepSeek V4 Flash access here, so nothing was reproduced. The lowest-threshold move: give your own agent a 'self-modification record' — persist what changed in each prompt/tool/procedure round plus the outcome, and feed it into the next improvement round. Better than tweaking prompts on vibes.
