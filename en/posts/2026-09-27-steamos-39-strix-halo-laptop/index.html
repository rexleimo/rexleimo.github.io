SteamOS 3.9 on a Gaming Laptop: Radeon 8060S iGPU hits 1440p AAA at 60+ FPS, 7h battery unplugged

Valve has never shipped a SteamOS laptop, but ETA Prime installed SteamOS 3.9 on a 2026 ASUS TUF A14 (Strix Halo / Radeon 8060S) and tested it: 1440p high …

September 27, 2026 · 5 min · 1004 words · Dreambeast -  |  中文

TL;DR#

Valve still hasn’t shipped a SteamOS laptop in 2026, but SteamOS 3.9 is now installable on non-Valve AMD PCs. ETA Prime took a 2026 ASUS TUF Gaming A14 (AMD Strix Halo / Ryzen AI Max+ 392 + Radeon 8060S iGPU) and benchmarked it. Two numbers to anchor on: 1440p high settings on AAA titles like Cyberpunk 2077 and Marvel’s Spider-Man 2 with an iGPU, and 1.7-7 hours of unplugged battery life.

I cross-checked that data against NotebookCheck and VideoCardz, both of which corroborate it, and I found one detail the secondary coverage glossed over. Below I go performance → battery → why this matters more than the laptop itself.

The full per-game frame rates and battery numbers live in ETA Prime’s original video, embedded below (YouTube, plays normally):

What the machine is#

Not a Valve laptop — Valve has never shipped one. It’s the 2026 Asus TUF Gaming A14 FA401EA:

  • CPU: AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 392 (Strix Halo), 12 cores/24 threads Zen 5, 5.0GHz boost
  • GPU: Radeon 8060S iGPU, 40 RDNA 3.5 compute units, unified-memory architecture, sharing LPDDR5X with the CPU
  • RAM: 32GB LPDDR5X, with 16GB carved out as VRAM
  • Display: 2560x1600, up to 165Hz
  • Battery: 73Wh
  • OS: SteamOS 3.9 (Valve main-channel test build)

Strix Halo is the ceiling for mobile APUs: strong CPU, strong iGPU, restrained power. It’s made for “one machine that does both performance and battery.” This laptop sits right on that sweet spot.

Gaming: 1440p high, the iGPU holds up#

Everything at 1440p, high preset, frame generation (FG) off by default (a consistent test condition; FG nearly doubles FPS but isn’t the baseline). The cross-verified results across four titles:

Game1440p high settingsMeasured FPS
Halo: Campaign EvolvedHigh + FSR Medium (~Balanced)~70 FPS mid-game
Crimson DesertHigh + FSR Qualitysteady 60+ FPS
Marvel’s Spider-Man 2High, FSR fully offavg 80+ FPS
DOOM: The Dark AgesHigh + FSR (required)near 60 FPS
Cyberpunk 2077High + FSR 2.1 Qualitymostly 60, dips below in effect-heavy scenes

Two details worth calling out:

  • Spider-Man 2 is a redemption arc. It used to be a Linux laggard (dropped Steam Deck Verified), and recent updates pulled it up — high settings, no FSR, still averaging 80+. That’s not just FPS, it’s a signal that “the Linux ecosystem is catching up.”
  • DOOM needs FSR to hold 60. An iGPU running a demanding title like DOOM clearly drops FPS without FSR — both secondary sources flagged this. It’s an honest footnote that “iGPU AAA still has trade-offs.”

Bottom line: the iGPU isn’t being oversold as a dGPU, but at 1440p high, every one of these AAA titles stays above the “playable” line.

Battery: how far unplugged actually goes#

This is where Strix Halo + SteamOS really lands. All of it on SteamOS’s default power policy, no TDP or power tuning (the original author states this explicitly — i.e. the “no-tinker” baseline):

  • Indie games (e.g. Hollow Knight: Silksong, 1200p @ 60FPS): whole system held under 16W, estimated 4-7 hours depending on load
  • AAA (Cyberpunk 2077, Steam Deck preset @ 60FPS): ~2h25m at 800p, dropping to ~1h40m at 1200p (73Wh)

A whole system at 16W on an indie game is rare for a gaming laptop. 7 hours means a single flight or day out covers a portable-console session on indies; 3A two-plus hours is fine for a commute or short trip.

Honest note (where the secondary sources diverge): the WeChat re-POST lists DOOM: The Dark Ages at “1440p high + FSR Balanced hits 89 FPS, near 90.” But NotebookCheck and VideoCardz agree on “DOOM needs FSR to hold near 60.” Root of the gap: the 89 FPS version allocated 16GB VRAM to the Radeon 8060S before testing — when VRAM is comfortable, the frame rate climbs; “must enable FSR to hold 60” is the result when VRAM is tight or the test is stricter. Both are correct; it depends how much VRAM you give the iGPU. I keep both sides as-is rather than dropping one.

Why “SteamOS open to regular PCs” beats “Valve ships a laptop”#

ETA Prime’s original title is “The One PC Valve Forgot.” NotebookCheck pushes the conclusion up a gear: in Valve’s Steam Machine plan, the most valuable output might not be the hardware itself, but the official opening of SteamOS to non-Valve PCs.

The logic:

  1. Before, recreating the Steam Deck experience on a gaming laptop meant tinkering with Bazzite or similar gaming Linux builds, or a non-official install path — high barrier, community-maintained
  2. SteamOS 3.8/3.9 broadened hardware support, so an officially supported AMD PC is a one-liner to install, getting the same software experience as the Steam Deck / Steam Machine
  3. The hardware bar isn’t high: 6 cores/12 threads + RDNA 2/3 or newer gets you in; top-end Strix Halo is “over-qualified”

In other words, Valve effectively turned a “gaming-specific Linux” into a self-buildable OS, and the community beat Valve’s own hardware to proving out “SteamOS laptops.” For gamers, that’s the real increment.

Should you buy / tinker#

Three takeaways:

  • Pure gaming, want battery + zero hassle: Strix Halo + SteamOS is the smoothest “portable console” of 2026 — 1440p high on AAA, hours unplugged, it holds up
  • Mixed use / Windows software: don’t sacrifice a general-purpose machine for SteamOS, or go dual-OS, because 3.9 is still a test build with no update channel
  • Budget / alternative: NotebookCheck notes the same money (~$1,999) buys a 32GB+1TB TUF A14, or a faster same-price GMKtec EVO-X2 mini PC (Ryzen AI Max+ 395 + 64GB) — mini for a fixed setup, laptop for mobility, pick by scenario

Data point: ETA Prime original + NotebookCheck (2026-08-04) + VideoCardz cross-check, pulled 2026-09-27. SteamOS 3.9 is a test build; stable-release numbers may move.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I install SteamOS on my own laptop?

Yes, with hardware constraints. The official minimum is a 6-core/12-thread CPU plus an RDNA 2/3 or newer Radeon GPU. The laptop in this article is an AMD Strix Halo (Ryzen AI Max+ 392 + Radeon 8060S); machines without an AMD GPU are not in official support scope.

Is SteamOS 3.9 a release?

No. VideoCardz notes 3.9 is still a test build on Valve's main channel, hosted on Valve's own servers, with GitHub mainly for issue reporting and Valve not yet providing an update channel. The numbers here are test-build results and may change in a stable release.

How long does the battery last?

Two tiers: indie games (1200p @ 60FPS, whole system under 16W) ~4-7 hours; AAA Cyberpunk 2077 (Steam Deck preset @ 60FPS) ~2h25m at 800p, ~1h40m at 1200p. 73Wh battery, SteamOS default power policy, no TDP tuning.

Is it worth it over Windows?

For a pure gaming setup, SteamOS's desktop mode (KDE) plus its console-style UI is a genuine win, and this Strix Halo config runs the same games at essentially the same frame rates under Windows (the iGPU takes the same Vulkan/driver path). SteamOS's added value is battery life and an unbothered gaming experience; for mixed use, Windows stays more general-purpose.
